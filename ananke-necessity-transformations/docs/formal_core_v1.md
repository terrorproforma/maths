# Formal core of the v1 obstruction

This note isolates the theorem-level content from the statistical and numerical
approximations.

## Definition 1 — behavioural process

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite operation alphabet and

\[
f:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb R
\]

an exact scalar behaviour. Its behavioural Hankel operator is

\[
H_f(u,v)=f(uv).
\]

Assume \(H_f\) has finite rank \(r\). A minimal linear realization is

\[
f(w)=\alpha B_w\omega,
\qquad
B_w=B_{w_1}\cdots B_{w_k},
\]

with state-space dimension \(r\).

## Definition 2 — finite classical stochastic realization

An \(N\)-state classical realization consists of

\[
p\in\Delta_N,
\qquad
M_a\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{N\times N},
\qquad
M_a\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1,
\qquad
0\le e\le\mathbf 1,
\]

such that

\[
f(w)=pM_w e
\]

for every word \(w\).

## Proposition 1 — spectral inheritance

If \(f\) has an \(N\)-state classical realization and
\((\alpha,\{B_a\},\omega)\) is its minimal linear realization, then for every
operation \(a\),

\[
\operatorname{spec}(B_a)\subseteq\operatorname{spec}(M_a).
\]

### Proof sketch

Let \(R\) be the joint reachable subspace generated by

\[
pM_w,\qquad w\in\Sigma^*.
\]

It is invariant under right multiplication by every \(M_a\). Let \(U\subseteq R\)
be the jointly unobservable subspace

\[
U=\{x\in R:xM_w e=0\text{ for all }w\in\Sigma^*\}.
\]

It is likewise invariant. The minimal realization is isomorphic to the induced
action on the quotient \(R/U\). In a basis adapted first to \(R\), then to \(U\),
the matrix of \(M_a\) is block upper triangular and the quotient block is similar to
\(B_a\). Hence the characteristic polynomial of \(B_a\) divides that of the relevant
restriction, and every eigenvalue of \(B_a\) is an eigenvalue of \(M_a\). \(\square\)

## Proposition 2 — Karpelevič state-count obstruction

Let \(\Theta_N\) denote the set of all eigenvalues of \(N\times N\) stochastic
matrices. If a minimal transition \(B_a\) has an eigenvalue

\[
\lambda\notin\Theta_N,
\]

then \(f\) has no exact classical stochastic realization with \(N\) states.

### Proof

Otherwise Proposition 1 would place \(\lambda\) in the spectrum of an
\(N\times N\) stochastic matrix, contradicting the definition of \(\Theta_N\).
\(\square\)

Because \(\Theta_N\subseteq\Theta_{N+1}\), exclusion through order \(N\) implies

\[
N_{\mathrm{classical}}\ge N+1.
\]

## Proposition 3 — confidence-set version

Suppose a random set \(C(D)\subset\mathbb C\), constructed from data \(D\), satisfies

\[
\Pr\bigl(\lambda\in C(D)\bigr)\ge1-\alpha
\]

for a selected minimal-transition eigenvalue \(\lambda\). If the observed set obeys

\[
C(D)\cap\Theta_N=\varnothing,
\]

then, with coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), no exact \(N\)-state classical stochastic
realization exists.

### Qualification

This proposition transfers whatever coverage the eigenvalue set actually has. V1's
plug-in bootstrap disk is an approximation to such a set; the proposition does not
itself prove its coverage after rank and eigenmode selection.

## Definition 3 — exact ontology cost

For a realization class \(\mathcal O\), define

\[
C_{\mathcal O}(f)
=
\inf\{N:f\text{ has an exact size-}N\text{ realization in }\mathcal O\}.
\]

For finite classical stochastic models, Proposition 2 gives

\[
C_{\mathrm{classical}}(f)
\ge
1+\max\{N:\operatorname{spec}(B_a)\cap
(\mathbb C\setminus\Theta_N)\ne\varnothing
\text{ for some }a\}.
\]

## Definition 4 — approximate ontology-cost profile

Choose an operational discrepancy over a word family \(\mathcal W\), for example

\[
d_{\mathcal W}(f,g)
=
\sup_{w\in\mathcal W}|f(w)-g(w)|.
\]

Then define

\[
C_{\mathcal O}(f;\varepsilon,\mathcal W)
=
\inf\left\{
N:\exists g_N\in\mathcal O_N,
\ d_{\mathcal W}(f,g_N)\le\varepsilon
\right\}.
\]

This is the proposed **counterfeit-complexity function**. It asks how much machinery
an alternative ontology must pay to imitate the observed process at precision
\(\varepsilon\).

## Proposition 4 — elementary properties

For fixed \(f,\mathcal O,\mathcal W\):

1. \(C_{\mathcal O}(f;\varepsilon,\mathcal W)\) is nonincreasing in
   \(\varepsilon\).
2. If \(\mathcal W_1\subseteq\mathcal W_2\), then

   \[
   C_{\mathcal O}(f;\varepsilon,\mathcal W_1)
   \le
   C_{\mathcal O}(f;\varepsilon,\mathcal W_2).
   \]

3. Exact cost is the zero-error limit whenever the infimum is attained and the model
   classes are closed in the chosen operational topology.

These are immediate from inclusion of feasible realization sets.

## Arithmetic upper pressure from finite cycles

For a peripheral mode

\[
\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha},
\]

an order-\(q\) deterministic cycle supplies the root

\[
\zeta_{p/q}=e^{2\pi i p/q}.
\]

Therefore the spectral distance to the nearest available periodic counterfeit of
order at most \(N\) is

\[
\delta_N(\alpha)
=
\min_{1\le q\le N}\min_{p\in\mathbb Z}
\left|e^{2\pi i\alpha}-e^{2\pi i p/q}\right|.
\]

Its change points are controlled by continued-fraction convergents and intermediate
fractions of \(\alpha\). This does not by itself construct a classical realization of
the entire multi-operation behaviour, but it gives a necessary arithmetic component
of any peripheral spectral imitation.

## Conjecture — noble phase design

Fix a small phase sector beginning with continued-fraction coefficient \(k\), and
consider irrational phases

\[
\alpha=[0;k,a_2,a_3,\ldots].
\]

Among phases in that sector, noble tails

\[
\alpha_*=[0;k,\overline{1}]
\]

are conjectured to maximize an asymptotic lower envelope of the finite-shot
classical-memory witness, after optimizing preparation and measurement for stable
mode extraction.

This is stronger than the classical statement that noble numbers are badly
approximable: the Karpelevič boundary curves, joint transition tuple and statistical
condition number also enter. It is the central v2 theorem target, not a result of v1.
